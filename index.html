The First Spark

The story

When I was in high school, I was looking for an old photo of my cousin on my father’s phone. After searching through the gallery two or three times, I still couldn’t find it. My cousin found it in just 20 seconds. I was amazed when he showed me Google Photos’ face recognition feature, which automatically groups pictures of the same person into a collection.

It was the first time I saw something so fascinating that it sparked a question in my mind: how can machines recognize people? Some time later, I learned that this technology is part of computer vision.

Rehab Gaze Tracker

The story

Galanto Innovations is a health-tech startup that builds game-based active rehabilitation devices for stroke and paralysis patients.

Stroke and paralysis patients can have difficulty maintaining their attention during rehabilitation therapy because of the pain and physical effort involved. They may look away, talk to other patients, or lose focus during their exercises, which can affect their engagement with the therapy.

To address this challenge, I built a complete MediaPipe-based eye and gaze detection and tracking system for stroke and paralysis patients. It tracks gaze direction, blink rate, and eye closure, and triggers an audio alert to notify the therapist when patients look away or stop engaging with their game-based active rehabilitation exercises.

MirrorMotion

The story

While working at Galanto Innovations, I learned how difficult even small movements can be for stroke and paralysis patients. I wanted to build something that could help make their recovery process more engaging.

After completing my internship tasks, I learned the Unity game engine to develop an interactive rehabilitation game that responds to movements in the real world. Patients hold a physical ball and move it forward, backward, left, or right. A virtual ball on the screen mirrors their movements, allowing them to hit boxes and earn points. As patients progress through increasingly challenging levels, the game supports their rehabilitation exercises.

I provided the game to the startup for integration into its rehabilitation device. This web version replaces the physical ball with a fist tracked by the camera.

This positive thought of helping patients led me to develop a new skill in game development.

GourdDetect

The story

Sponge gourd, ridge gourd, cucumber and bottle gourd look so alike that many people my age, me included, can’t tell them apart.

Dataset: For my minor project, I went to the vegetable market and, with the vendors’ permission, photographed carts with three or four gourd species in a single frame. I added images from Google and open datasets, then drew a bounding box around every vegetable by hand.

I trained YOLOv8 on this dataset. It reached very good accuracy, and the project led to a conference paper.

Which vegetable is this?

Cucumber, sponge gourd, ridge gourd and bottle gourd sit side by side, all long and green. How would you know which one to buy?

I couldn’t tell them apart either, and neither could many people my age. So for my minor project during my bachelor’s, I built GourdDetect, a system that detects and recognises visually similar vegetables in a real market.

Stereo Depth

The story

As a member of Cretus, the robotics club during my bachelor’s, I was preparing a presentation for a Mars Rover workshop when I learned that rovers use a camera setup called stereo vision.

While exploring it, I found it is also used in autonomous vehicles. So I learned monocular and stereo vision and built a depth estimation system with two cameras. The setup was hard, because I couldn’t afford two identical cameras.

It measures the distance of a ball from the cameras, with the idea that if cars on the road come too close, the system could beep.

AR Ruler

The story

At a store, I found a table I liked for our home. My father asked me to check it would fit, but its measurement label was torn and the staff were rushing to close.

My friend measured it with Apple’s Measure app, and I was amazed. Later he measured a 15 cm scale, and it was right. I searched for how it works but couldn’t find a clear explanation.

So during my vacation I built my own tool that computes lengths, rectangle and triangle areas, and a circle’s radius, diameter, area and volume from points the user selects.

Hack For India

ARtist
Empowering traditional artisans through augmented reality

Problem we solve

India’s rich artistic heritage spans traditional crafts and art forms from Kashmir to Kanyakumari. However, many traditional artisans struggle with limited visibility, low demand, and restricted access to potential customers. These challenges can force skilled artists to leave their professions, threatening cultural traditions and limiting their economic opportunities.

Our solution

During Hack For India, organized by Silver Oak University in partnership with Indian Oil, our team developed ARtist, an online marketplace and artistic community designed to connect traditional artisans with art enthusiasts. The platform enables artists to showcase and sell their work, share the stories behind their creations, and organize expos or traditional fairs to reach wider audiences.

A key feature I developed was an augmented reality (AR) visualization pipeline that lets buyers virtually place artworks in their own spaces before purchasing. This creates a more immersive shopping experience and helps customers understand how an artwork might fit into their surroundings.

Impact and recognition

ARtist aimed to improve the visibility of traditional artisans, expand their access to potential customers, and help preserve India’s artistic heritage through technology. The project was appreciated by mentors and experts for identifying an overlooked social challenge and exploring an innovative, technology-driven solution.

Smart India Hackathon

BhashaSetu
Multilingual speech-to-text and translation system

Problem we solve

Government agencies, security organizations, and research institutions often handle audio recordings in multiple regional languages. Manually understanding, transcribing, and translating these recordings is time-consuming and creates barriers to efficient information processing and communication.

Our solution

For the Smart India Hackathon, we took on a problem statement from the National Technical Research Organisation (NTRO). In just two days, our team built a prototype that transcribes Hindi, Gujarati, and Bengali speech into English text. Our pipeline included audio preprocessing, such as noise reduction and silence removal, and fine-tuning IndicWav2Vec to better handle the characteristics of regional languages.

Potential impact

The system could support multilingual communication, make spoken information accessible to people who are deaf through text transcription, and help media organizations transcribe regional-language interviews and news reports for wider dissemination.
